Design review before build: three mockup variants of the "Cash overview" screen of Larkspur, a made-up B2B finance analytics web app for CFOs and finance managers. One variant will be built in code next.

The brief the mockups were made from:
- Navigation "Overview", "Cash", "Invoices", "Forecast", "Reports", "Settings" ("Cash" active); title "Cash overview"; period "Last 12 months"; button "Export report".
- KPI cards: "Cash on hand" "$4.82M" "+6.1% vs last month"; "Monthly burn" "$312K"; "Runway" "15.4 months"; "Days sales outstanding" "41 days" "-3 days".
- Chart "Cash in vs cash out", 12 months "Dec" to "Nov".
- Table "Overdue invoices": Northwind Freight $48,200 34 days M. Kowalska; Brightline Retail $31,750 21 days J. Nowak; Orbis Medical $12,900 12 days A. Lis; Kestrel Foods $9,400 7 days M. Kowalska.
- Variant 3 only: an "Alerts" panel with "Runway below 18 months" and "3 invoices over 30 days".
- Palette: background #F5F7FA, navigation #0F2A44, accent #2F5BFF, positive #1F9D6B, negative #D64545.

The mockups are these image files. Open and look at each one before you judge:

Review them as a senior product designer and a finance user would: does each screen tell the truth (do the numbers, chart, colours and alerts agree with each other), is the meaning of colour right for finance (good news vs bad news), hierarchy, density, legibility, and anything a CFO would distrust. Check the brief itself too: if the brief contains a contradiction, say so. Rank the three, name the variant to build, and list what must change in it.
